# Galaxis on Fire

**Current prepared build:** `v0.2.0-alpha+build.3` ? Arms, Armor and Night Encounters. See [Versions and Patch Notes](Versions-and-Patch-Notes) for changes and verification.

**Chart a star system. Land. Survive. Push deeper.**

Welcome to the **Galaxis on Fire** wiki!

Galaxis on Fire is a 2D pixel-art sandbox adventure built around planetary exploration, survival, building, technology, and magic. Explore a procedurally generated galaxy, establish a foothold on unfamiliar worlds, and turn the resources around you into the equipment you need for your next expedition.

Your journey begins with a crashed escape pod and a damaged ascent drive. Your main ship is still in orbit, and your robot companion, **R0-VR**, is there to help you get back to it. Gather supplies, build a workshop, grow food, improve your equipment, and investigate a buried signal to recover what you need for repairs. Once the pod is ready, return to your ship and continue exploring other planets.

## A galaxy to explore

Each expedition starts with a customizable character and a world seed. Travel through star systems and discover planets with different climates, terrain, resources, and creatures. Forests and grasslands give way to deserts, island chains, broad oceans, frozen seas, tundra, and rocky or volcanic landscapes.

Exploration continues beneath the surface. Deep caves contain ores, glowing crystals, underground biomes, water and lava pools, dangerous creatures, and structures to investigate. The supplies you bring can matter as much as the equipment you hope to find.

## Build your way forward

- **Mine and craft:** Gather materials, smelt ores, and progress through stronger equipment. Tool types and mining tiers determine how effectively you can work different materials.
- **Make a home:** Build shelters with doors, ladders, furniture, lighting, storage, and crafting stations with their own inventories.
- **Farm and cook:** Till soil, plant and water crops, harvest produce, and prepare food for the next trip underground.
- **Fight and adapt:** Use melee weapons, bows, arrows, and magic against surface, flying, and cave creatures.
- **Create magical equipment:** Learn connected skills and use alchemy desks and cauldrons to combine unlocked effects into custom wands, staffs, and tomes. Explore destruction, restoration, wards, illumination, and necromancy—including raised allies that fight alongside you.
- **Build powered systems:** Connect generators, poles, wiring, switches, lights, and logic devices to create electrical systems.

Weather and terrain are part of the world you live in. Wind moves vegetation, rain can leave puddles, snow accumulates and melts, and water and lava flow through open terrain. Digging, building, and changing the ground can affect what happens around you.

## Explore together

Choose **Multiplayer** from the main menu to host or join an expedition. Hosts can start a new game or load an existing Godot save. Guests can find LAN games in the server browser or connect using an IP address or hostname.

Co-op supports sessions of up to eight captains. Each player has separate equipment and character progress, while the host manages the shared world, saves, and planetary travel. Multiplayer is an early alpha feature; broader testing across different computers and network setups is still underway.

## Starting your first expedition

Create your character, choose a seed or let the game generate one, and begin on a temperate forest planet. Find R0-VR near the crash site and follow the repair objectives. Start with supplies and a workshop, establish shelter and food, then prepare for the journey underground.

**Your first goal is to repair the pod. Where you go after that is up to you.**

# Getting Started

Your first goal in Galaxis on Fire is to survive a crash, repair your escape pod, and return to the main ship in orbit.

## Create an expedition

1. Choose **New Game**. For co-op, choose **Multiplayer**, then **Host Game** and start a new expedition.
2. Customize your character and enter a captain name.
3. Enter a seed to choose a repeatable galaxy, or leave it blank for a random seed.
4. Select a star system and a temperate forest planet. Normal expedition progression begins on this planet type.

New characters begin with empty inventories and hotbars. Gather supplies and earn equipment through crafting and expedition objectives.

## Find your bearings

- **HP and MP:** Health and mana share the upper-left status panel. Your held item is listed underneath.
- **World information:** The upper-right panel shows the day, time, weather, forecast, and location information.
- **Hotbar:** Select items with **1–9**, **Tab**, or the mouse wheel.
- **Expedition objectives:** Press **J** to expand or collapse the objective panel.

## Your first supplies

Find **R0-VR**, the robot near the crash site, and press **E** or **F** to talk. Follow the objectives to gather wood, craft planks and sticks, and make a workbench.

Place the workbench from your hotbar and press **F** while aiming at it. Put ingredients into its own inventory slots to craft your first equipment. A shovel works well on soil, an axe on trees, and a pickaxe on stone and ores.

## Before going underground

Establish a workshop, shelter, lighting, and a food supply. Bring a weapon, useful tools, torches, and food. Caves are dark and can contain creatures, water, unstable material, and lava.

Save with **F5**. In multiplayer, the host manages the save.

Continue with [Expedition and R0-VR](Expedition-and-R0-VR), [Controls](Controls), and [Crafting and Stations](Crafting-and-Stations).

# Character Creation

Character creation lets you choose your captain's appearance before starting an expedition.

## Appearance options

Customize the character model, skin tone, hairstyle and hair color, eye color, shirt style and color, pants style and color, and shoe style and color. Male and female character models are available.

Use the preview to inspect the character and its animations. The random appearance option provides a starting point that you can adjust afterward. Character artwork uses the game's Aseprite pixel-art assets.

## Name and seed

Enter a captain name and choose a galaxy seed. These serve different purposes: the name identifies your captain, while the seed selects the initial generated galaxy and planets.

Numeric and text seeds are accepted. Leave the seed blank to generate a random one. See [World Generation and Seeds](World-Generation-and-Seeds).

## Starting equipment

A new expedition starts with an empty inventory and hotbar. Appearance choices do not grant equipment. Gather materials, craft tools, and follow R0-VR's objectives to develop your character.

## Multiplayer identity

The join menu lets you enter a captain name and uses the current character appearance. The host keeps each guest's equipment and progress separately.

Reconnecting characters are identified by a saved player identity, rather than the display name alone. See [Multiplayer](Multiplayer) before moving or replacing your user-data files.

# Expedition and R0-VR

Your escape pod has crashed, its ascent drive needs repairs, and the main ship is still in orbit. **R0-VR**, your robot companion, guides the first expedition.

Press **E** or **F** near R0-VR to talk. Use **J** to expand or collapse the expedition objective panel. Return to the robot when an objective is ready to complete.

## Repair progression

| Stage | Main objective |
| --- | --- |
| Meet the robot | Speak to R0-VR and begin the repair effort. |
| Gather supplies | Collect wood, establish basic crafting, and obtain the tools needed for stone. |
| Set up a workshop | Place a workbench and furnace and improve your equipment. |
| Shelter and food | Build a lit shelter and harvest your first corn crop. |
| Copper equipment | Smelt copper and craft copper tools and a weapon. |
| The buried signal | Investigate the underground signal and recover the guarded drive core. |

Prepare before following the signal underground. Bring an appropriate pickaxe, a weapon, food, and lighting. The drive core is guarded by a sentinel, so reaching its location is only part of the task.

## Leaving the planet

Once repairs and progression permit departure, press **G**, or interact with the pod using **F**, to return to orbit. The larger ship remains above the planet; the repaired pod provides the way back.

In co-op, the repair effort belongs to the shared expedition and the host manages planetary travel.

# Galaxy and Space Travel

The galaxy and solar-system views connect your planetary expeditions. Stars and planets are generated from the expedition seed.

## Choose a destination

Click a star in the galaxy view to open its solar system. Select a planet to inspect it, then use the landing button or **Enter / E** to land. Number keys **1–8** select planets in the system view.

Use **Esc** to return from the system view to the galaxy view. Surface travel back to orbit uses **G**, or an appropriate interaction at the repaired pod.

## Your first planet

A normal new expedition begins on a **temperate forest planet**. R0-VR's objectives guide you through repairing the crashed pod before you can visit other planets.

The pod is your ascent vehicle. The larger ship remains in orbit; repairing the pod lets you return to it and expand your exploration.

## What makes planets different?

Planet information includes climate and orbital characteristics. Different worlds can have different moons and rings. The surface sky reflects the selected system's star, along with the planet's moons and rings where applicable.

Temperate forest planets are placed within their star's habitable zone. Planet climates and their surface biomes affect the landscape, vegetation, wildlife, resources, and weather you encounter.

## Returning to explored worlds

Visited worlds are retained in the expedition's save, including terrain edits and structures. Returning to a planet continues that saved world rather than creating a fresh copy from its seed.

In co-op, the host chooses travel destinations and the group follows to the next landing site. Guests retain their inventories.

# World Generation and Seeds

Galaxis on Fire creates galaxies, solar systems, and planetary terrain procedurally. A seed determines the starting layout of an expedition.

## Choosing a seed

- Enter a **number** for a numeric seed.
- Enter **text** to generate a seed from that text.
- Leave the field **blank** for a random seed.

The same seed and generation version provide a repeatable starting layout. Major world-generation changes between builds can change what a seed produces.

## What is generated?

Generation includes star systems, planet climates, surface biome regions, terrain, resources, caves, vegetation, and creature populations. Underground content includes multiple cave environments, pools, crystals, and structures.

Planet types contain their own regional biomes. A forest planet can include open meadows and rocky uplands as well as dense woodland.

Ocean worlds check connected basins and caves during loading. This helps account for caves that should already contain water when the world first opens. Frozen-ocean terrain can include tall glacial spires.

## Seeds and saves are different

A seed describes the starting world. A save also records your expedition state, altered terrain, constructions, inventory, and other progress.

Starting again with the same seed does not recreate your previous buildings or recover items from an older save. Keep the save file when preserving an expedition.

## Reporting generation problems

Include the game version, seed, planet type, approximate location, and a screenshot when reporting a misplaced structure, disconnected pool, unreachable area, or other generation problem. A save is helpful when the problem appeared after terrain was changed.

# Planets and Biomes

A planet's climate sets its overall character, while regional biomes change the terrain and scenery along the surface. Forest planets have open areas as well as wooded regions; other climates have their own biome sets.

## Planet types

| Planet type | Surface biomes |
| --- | --- |
| Temperate forest and grasslands | Meadow, Woodland, Dense forest, Rocky uplands |
| Scattered islands and shallow sea | Sandy shore, Coastal grove, Island jungle, Tidal banks |
| Mixed oceans and continents | Coastal grassland, Continental forest, Rocky coast, Sandbars |
| Hot desert and dunes | Dune sea, Rocky badlands, Oasis, Stony desert |
| Rocky inner world and lava plains | Basalt plains, Lava fields, Obsidian flats, Volcanic ridges |

Terrain materials, relief, tree density, flora, wildlife, and liquids vary with climate and biome. Biome backgrounds provide additional depth and scenery as you travel.

Frozen oceans feature ice formations and towering spires. Volcanic inner worlds contain lava landscapes and ash or ember weather instead of ordinary water rain.

## Underground biomes

Caves have a separate set of environments, ranging from root caverns and aquifers to crystal, fungal, basalt, and molten regions. Surface climate does not describe every underground location.

# Underground Exploration

Below the surface is a deep cave network with resources, lighting hazards, creatures, liquids, and generated structures. Surface entrances can slope into the ground rather than always forming vertical shafts.

## Underground environments

The cave biome set includes:

- Root caverns
- Limestone halls
- Sunken aquifer
- Prism caverns
- Fungal hollows
- Basalt depths
- Molten underworld

Glowing crystals appear in several colors and provide local illumination. Cave scenery includes hanging formations, stalagmites, drips, and vines. Water and lava pools occur underground, with substantial lava near the deepest region.

## Prepare a loadout

Bring an appropriate pickaxe, a weapon, healing food, and lighting. Ladders and building materials can help maintain a return route. A Hammer or Mattock gives access to supported background deposits.

Mining is limited to four blocks of reach. Higher-tier ores still require the correct harvest level; reaching a deposit does not mean your current pickaxe can collect it.

## Background ores

Some deposits sit on the cave background rather than occupying a normal foreground block. Use a **Hammer** or **Mattock** to work these seams. More demanding seams require the Mattock.

Normal underground ore artwork is distinct from the element-style inventory icons.

## Structures and hazards

The lower world can contain block-built bridges over lava and underground fortress structures. Check the surrounding terrain and liquids before making alterations.

Water can drain into connected caves, and digging into an aquifer can open a new flow path. Unsupported sand and gravel can fall. Lava damages the player and can form obsidian when interacting with water.

## The buried signal

The first expedition eventually directs you to a guarded drive core. Follow the objective information and prepare for its sentinel before attempting the recovery. Return to R0-VR and the crashed pod as the objectives instruct.

# Weather and World Mechanics

The world responds to weather and terrain changes. A tunnel, a broken support, or an opened basin can change how the surrounding area behaves.

## Time, skies, and weather

The information panel shows the day, time, current weather, forecast, and planetary context. Weather transitions blend gradually instead of switching instantly between fully clear and fully stormy conditions.

Weather includes clear skies, cloud cover, rain, storms, fog, and snow where the climate permits. Volcanic inner worlds replace water rain with ashfall, storms with ember storms, and fog with volcanic haze.

Cloud cover changes with the weather. Rain direction responds to wind strength and direction. Gusts affect vegetation, falling leaves, and tumbleweeds. The selected system's star influences the visible sun, and planetary moons and rings can appear in the sky.

## Water

Water flows into open areas and redistributes when terrain changes. Opening a path into a connected pool or aquifer can flood an excavation. Pressure behavior matters when making new connections between bodies of water.

Rain can form puddles that evaporate over time. Snow can accumulate, melt into water, and contribute runoff. Actors interacting with water can produce ripples and splashes.

Use Space, W, or Up to rise through water, and approach a clear bank when leaving it. Do not assume a pool's edge will remain unchanged after you dig into it.

## Lava and obsidian

Lava flows more slowly than water and damages the player. Contact between water and lava can form obsidian. Exposed lava can also solidify after sufficient cooling from rain where climate and exposure permit.

## Falling materials and regrowth

Unsupported sand and gravel can fall and settle as mineable terrain. Dirt and structural blocks do not all behave like falling sand.

Suitable exposed dirt can grow grass after digging. Natural foliage responds to wind, while trees and most plants remain passable scenery.

## Underground visibility

Deep areas become dark. Torches, fireplaces, powered lights, magical illumination, and glowing cave crystals provide different light sources. Surface fog is shaped around the exterior landscape rather than filling every underground cave.

# Flora and Trees

Surface vegetation is distributed by planet climate and regional biome. A temperate forest world includes open meadows, woodland clusters, dense forest, and rocky areas rather than one continuous wall of trees.

## Trees and ground cover

Trees, grass, flowers, ferns, shrubs, reeds, moss, lichen, and other plants are passable scenery. You can walk through natural vegetation instead of treating it as a solid terrain barrier.

Plants sway in the wind and can react as the player passes. Tree leaves fall with the wind. Tumbleweeds move with the wind and can travel over changes in ground height.

Bushes, thickets, mushrooms, pebble clusters, and boulders provide additional ground detail. Rocks are not restricted to one rocky surface biome.

## Gathering

Use an appropriate tool to harvest vegetation. Axes are the preferred tree-cutting tool and require multiple hits; a pickaxe is much less effective on a trunk.

Felled tree pieces drop from their original positions and settle toward the ground. Collect materials as they become available. Some surface plants also provide planting stock for crops.

## Desert flora

The desert plant set includes:

- Fruiting Opuntia cactus
- Joshua Tree
- Golden Barrel Cactus
- Saguaro Cactus
- Brittlebush
- Ghost Plant
- Fox Tail Agave
- Tumbleweed
- Desert Marigold
- Desert Lily
- Jade Plant
- Aloe Vera
- Chaste Tree
- Cane Cholla
- Mexican Fencepost Cactus

Other climates use vegetation suited to their environments, including frost plants, ice coral, crystal blooms, and ember fronds.

## Regrowth and decorating

Suitable exposed dirt can develop grass after terrain edits. Conditions such as climate and water affect where it can appear. Gathered ground-life items can also be used to decorate appropriate spaces.

Sprite sources for the game's flora are maintained in Aseprite.

# Creatures and Wildlife

World populations combine small environmental animals, planet-associated monsters, flying enemies, and underground creatures. A planet's climate and location influence what you encounter.

## Surface monsters

The main planet-associated monster families include **Torchcrest**, **Crown conch**, **Coraljaw**, **Veilrunner**, **Kiln beetle**, **Hammerhorn**, **Ruby tusker**, and **Plume strider**.

Their artwork, variants, movement, durability, and behavior differ. Do not assume a creature on a new planet will behave like one from the starting forest.

## Flying creatures

Flying enemy families include **Mosswing**, **Reed darter**, **Cloud ray**, **Sand hornet**, **Ash glider**, **Shardwing**, **Rime moth**, and **Dusk bat**. Some use warning, dive, and recovery behavior instead of simply walking toward the player.

Keep room to move, aim carefully, and consider ranged weapons when approaching aerial threats.

## Slimes and cave mobs

Slime types include Meadow, Brine, Dune, Rime, Cinder, and Prism varieties. Underground encounters also include cave scarabs, pale scuttlers, and cave screechers.

The buried drive-core objective has a **salvage sentinel** guarding it. Prepare equipment and supplies before investigating the signal.

## Night encounters

More hostile creatures emerge on the surface after sunset. Encounters build gradually through dusk and ease toward dawn, using the planet's ground monsters, slimes, and flying creatures.

Night visitors approach nearby players through the existing movement and collision systems. Keep a melee weapon ready and carry a bow or magic for flyers. These are additional encounters; underground creatures retain their existing biome populations.

New creatures appear outside every player's view, away from occupied spawn locations, liquids, and player-built background walls. Local and world population limits prevent endless accumulation. Distant night visitors leave after sunrise; visible enemies remain rather than disappearing during a fight. Retreating creatures do not drop loot or leave a corpse.

Existing saves support night encounters. In multiplayer, the host manages the population for everyone.

## Small fauna

Surface life includes beetles, butterflies, snails, sand hoppers, snow moths, ember mites, hares, and ground birds. These belong to the ground-life system and are different from the hostile monster roster.

Small fauna and vegetation help make regional biomes feel inhabited. Some ground-life items can be gathered and placed or released again.

## Necromancy

Fallen enemies can become a resource for the necromancy skill branch. Suitable corpses can be raised into temporary allies after the required research. A corpse that has already been raised cannot be reused.

# Farming and Food

Farming provides ingredients and healing food for exploration. Crops need suitable soil, moisture, and growing conditions.

## Grow your first crop

1. Obtain a **Hoe**, **Watering Can**, and seeds. R0-VR's early objectives help introduce farming equipment.
2. Equip the hoe and use it on grass or dirt with open space above.
3. Select seeds and right-click the tilled soil or the planting space above it.
4. Water the soil with the watering can. **V** is a shortcut when you own the can.
5. Keep the growing area moist and adequately lit.
6. Harvest the mature crop with a mining/interaction click.

Mature crops yield produce and planting stock. Removing an immature crop with a hoe recovers planting stock. Removing the soil underneath a crop also removes its support.

## Crops

| Crop | Planting item | Base growth work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corn | Crop Seeds | 150 seconds |
| Carrot | Carrot Seeds | 90 seconds |
| Potato | Seed Potato | 110 seconds |
| Tomato | Tomato Seeds | 140 seconds |
| Wheat | Wheat Seeds | 120 seconds |

These are base growth requirements, not guaranteed elapsed times. Dry soil, darkness, and climate change progress. Mature harvests normally provide 2–4 produce and 1–3 planting items.

Harvesting surface plants can provide seeds. Grasses/reeds, flowers, ferns/broadleaf plants, shrubs, and dry vegetation provide different planting stock.

Rain can water exposed soil. Nearby water can irrigate rows; solid barriers can interrupt that access. Soil dries over time, and dry crops grow more slowly.

# Power and Logic

Electrical systems combine generation, power distribution, and logic signals. A device can need both electrical supply and an enabling signal.

## Generators

| Generator | Nominal capacity | Accepted fuels |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Biomass Generator | 100 W | Raw Biomass, Wood, Leaves |
| Coal Generator | 300 W | Coal Ore |
| Fuel Generator | 600 W | Biofuel, Refined Fuel |

Place a generator, aim at it, and press **F** to open its fuel inventory. Put compatible fuel into the device. Capacity, consumption, and connected demand determine what the network can supply.

## Power wiring

Select **Wire** in the hotbar and right-click compatible gold power terminals to connect devices. Poles and connectors extend the network. Wooden power poles are eight blocks tall, with accessible ground-level connection points for overhead cables.

Cable spans are limited to **16 blocks**. Add a pole or connector when a destination is too far away. Press **Esc** to cancel a pending connection. Connecting an existing pair again can recover that cable.

A simple switched lamp circuit is:

1. Generator **P** to Power Switch **P**.
2. Power Switch **L** to the lamp's **P** terminal.
3. Fuel the generator and press **F** at the switch to toggle the connection.

## Lights and sensors

Electric Lamps, Wall Lights, Ceiling Lights, and Floodlights illuminate the world and can support suitable growing areas. Red Signal Lamps are indicators and do not provide crop-growing light.

Wall Switches toggle connections. Push Buttons produce short pulses. Pressure Plates respond to actors standing on them. Daylight Sensors detect exposed daylight; **F** toggles the sensor's inverted night mode.

## Logic wiring

Use **Logic Wire** for signals. Choose an output such as **Q** or **QB** first, then the destination input. Each input supports one driver. Ordinary power wiring does not substitute for a signal connection.

Logic devices need power. Logic Input can be toggled with **F**. Logic Output accepts **IN** and controls its output and power relay.

## Gates

| A | B | AND | OR | XOR | NAND | NOR |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LOW | LOW | LOW | LOW | LOW | HIGH | HIGH |
| LOW | HIGH | LOW | HIGH | HIGH | HIGH | LOW |
| HIGH | LOW | LOW | HIGH | HIGH | HIGH | LOW |
| HIGH | HIGH | HIGH | HIGH | LOW | LOW | LOW |

**NOT** reverses its A input.

## Memory devices

- **RS NOR Latch:** S/R are active HIGH. Both LOW holds the previous state; both HIGH is invalid.
- **RS NAND Latch:** S/R are active LOW. Both HIGH holds the previous state; both LOW is invalid.
- **T Flip-Flop:** A rising CLK edge toggles the stored state when T is HIGH. Holding CLK HIGH does not repeatedly toggle it.

Inspect device inputs, outputs, fuel, and network supply when a circuit does not behave as expected.

# Necromancy and Allies

Necromancy combines soul drain, decay effects, and temporary allies raised from fallen creatures.

## Research path

| Skill | Requirement and effect |
| --- | --- |
| Soul study | Rank 1 entry skill; unlocks Drain and bone-fragment progression |
| Blight | Rank 2, after Soul study; unlocks decay over time |
| Grave calling | Rank 2, after Soul study; unlocks raising a fallen ally |
| Spectral legion | Rank 4, after Grave calling; increases the summon limit to three |

Research also requires an available skill point. Open **K** to inspect the connected tree.

## Raise an ally

1. Learn Grave calling and obtain a Grave Tome or a supported custom Raise Dead focus.
2. Find a suitable dead creature that has not already been raised.
3. Move within range and aim at the corpse with a clear path.
4. Cast with enough mana and available summon capacity.

The corpse is marked as used when raising succeeds. It cannot be repeatedly raised for more allies. A blocked or crowded summon attempt does not consume that corpse.

## Capacity and duration

The normal limit is **one** raised ally. Spectral legion increases the limit to **three**. Allies are temporary; duration depends on the spell and is capped at 60 seconds for a single summon.

The remaining duration appears above the ally. The summon artwork varies with the magical focus, including a servitor, wraith, or hound.

## Following and fighting

Allies follow their owner, target visible enemies, and attack without harming the owner. When too far behind, they can recall to an available nearby position.

Summons check each other's occupied space when spawning and moving. They use separate follow/recall positions instead of stacking into one location, including beside another co-op captain's allies. Nearby Ally labels stagger to remain readable.

## Saving

Active summon state and remaining lifetime are included in character saves. On restoration, legal open positions are checked. Older saves with stacked allies are repositioned when space is available; invalid or unusable positions are not forced into the terrain.

# Multiplayer

Multiplayer is shared-world co-op. The host owns the expedition, saves, and planetary travel, while guests have separate character equipment and progress. Sessions support up to **eight captains**, including the host.

## Host an expedition

1. Choose **Multiplayer** from the main menu and open **Host Game**.
2. Enter a server name and game UDP port. The default is **24560**.
3. Choose a new game, the current single-player save, or a compatible Godot save file.
4. Select **Host Expedition** and make the Windows Firewall choice if prompted.
5. For a new game, complete character creation and choose the starting planet. Connected guests wait until a planet is ready.

New co-op games use a separate multiplayer save. Loading an existing file makes that file the host's save destination during the session. Host menus keep the shared world running.

## Join

Open the **Join Game** tab and enter a captain name. LAN servers appear in the browser with their address, player count, build version, and readiness.

Select a server and choose **Join Selected / Direct IP**, or double-click it. For a direct connection, enter the host's IP address or hostname and game port. **Save Address** keeps an address for later use. **Cancel Connection** exits a connection attempt or world-preparation wait.

The host and guest must use matching game builds. The browser discovers local-network games; it is not a public internet matchmaking directory.

## Windows Firewall

The permission dialog offers **Allow (Windows UAC)** or **Decline / Continue**.

- Allow requests administrator approval for inbound UDP rules for the current game executable.
- Rules use the selected game port and the next port for discovery: defaults **24560** and **24561**.
- Private network access is the default. Public network access is optional and initially unchecked.
- Discovery is restricted to the local subnet.
- Decline continues without making firewall changes; connections may be blocked unless access is already permitted.

Use **Windows Firewall...** in the multiplayer menu to revisit the choice. Created rules remain until removed or disabled in Windows Firewall.

## Internet connections

Firewall permission does not configure a router. Internet hosting needs a reachable host and router configuration for the selected **game UDP port**. Guests can then join using a direct address. LAN discovery is not opened to the internet.

## Progress and disconnecting

The host saves guest inventories, hotbars, durability, health, and magic progress. Reconnecting uses the saved player identity, not only the display name. Keep `multiplayer_identity.txt` in the game's user-data folder to retain that identity.

The host chooses planet travel, and guests follow with their equipment. Guests can disconnect from the session menu. Host shutdown returns guests to the browser. The host should save before ending the session.

This is an early co-op implementation for trusted groups. Cross-PC behavior, eight-player performance, and different router/firewall setups still need broader testing. PvP, public matchmaking, and automatic router port forwarding are not current features.

# Saves and Settings

A save records the expedition and its changed worlds. Settings store preferences such as presentation and sound levels.

## Saving and loading

- Press **F5** to save in single-player, or use the pause menu's save action.
- Use **Load Game** from the main menu to resume the normal save.
- **F9** is the single-player quick-load control where gameplay input permits it.
- **Save & Main Menu** and **Save & Quit** save before leaving through those actions.

The ordinary save is `galaxis_save.json` in Godot's game user-data directory (`user://`). It is separate from the source-code folder and from `version.json`.

## What is preserved?

Saved state includes the character and appearance, inventory and hotbar, tool wear, health, magical progression, expedition objectives, visited planets, terrain edits, and structures. Stations retain stored materials and supported processing state. Weather/time and relevant world-system state are also recorded.

A seed alone cannot restore this progress. Starting a fresh game with the same seed recreates a starting layout, not your previous constructions and equipment.

## Multiplayer saves

New hosted expeditions use `galaxis_multiplayer_save.json`. When the host loads a selected save file, that file becomes the session's save destination. Ending hosting restores the previous local save destination.

Only the host writes the shared world save. Guest character progress is stored there. The guest's `multiplayer_identity.txt` identifies the returning character; the display name alone does not select its saved progress.

## Settings

Settings tabs cover gameplay, video, audio, and control information. Options include day length, underground/night darkness preferences, fullscreen, and master/effects/ambience volumes.

During co-op, the session menu restricts loading another save and changing world settings. End the session before changing those aspects of the expedition.

## Keeping an expedition

Keep a separate copy of an important save before experimenting with developer edits or replacing game/user-data files. A Git source checkpoint is different from a player save; committing the project does not automatically preserve your `user://` expedition.

# Developer Mode

Developer mode provides tools for testing worlds, equipment, weather, and progression. It is useful for inspecting a planet type without completing the normal expedition first.

## Opening the panel

Press **F3** from a planet, the galaxy view, or a solar-system view. Press F3 again or use the appropriate close control to return to normal play.

In multiplayer, developer access belongs to the host. Guests cannot grant themselves items or use developer travel.

## Tabs

| Tab | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Weather | Inspect or change environmental conditions |
| Tools | Equipment/material grants and player testing controls |
| Farming | Farming supplies and related testing |
| Power | Electrical devices, wiring, and fuel supplies |
| Seasons | Date/season-related controls |
| Travel | Visit different planet climates for testing |
| Magic | Magical equipment, reagents, and spellcraft testing |

Testing controls include free flight and invulnerability options. Travel controls can bypass the normal pod-repair restrictions.

## Item commands

The command field supports commands such as:

```text
help
give Wood 64
give Copper Ore 20
give alltools
give all starter
```

Press **Enter** to execute. Item autocomplete is available with **Tab**. `give alltools` grants the defined starter tool bundle; its name does not mean every possible item or material tier is granted.

## Use a testing expedition

Granted items, changed terrain, and other world edits can be saved. Use a separate expedition when checking generation or experimenting with progression bypasses.

For a useful bug report, include the build version, seed, climate, location, and whether developer options were active.

## Reading the version

The prepared version `v0.2.0-alpha+build.3` contains:

- **0.2.0:** Release version: major, minor, and patch numbers.
- **alpha:** Development channel. Beta and release-candidate channels are also supported.
- **build.3:** The increasing build number for that checkpoint.

The game version appears in its main menu and window title. It is separate from the Godot engine's version. Multiplayer checks the game build when joining.

## Patch notes

[CHANGELOG.md](https://github.com/HeKontrolls/Galaxis_On_Fire_Godot/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md) contains the numbered build history and notes about additions, improvements, fixes, and verification.

The preceding build, `v0.1.0-alpha+build.2`, added hosting/joining, shared-world synchronization, guest saves and reconnection, the combined HP/MP panel, and improved raised-ally spacing.

## For contributors: changing build info

`version.json` in the project root holds the release version, build number, name, and date. The preparation helper synchronizes that information into Godot's runtime settings and the changelog.

From the project folder:

```powershell
powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File .\tools\Prepare-Build.ps1 -Message "Name of the next build"
```

Use `-Bump Patch`, `-Bump Minor`, or `-Bump Major` for a release-number change. Use `-ReuseVersion` when revising an already-prepared build without advancing its number again.

## Keeping changes ready to commit

Each completed change batch includes synchronized version/build metadata, numbered patch notes, affected wiki pages, and the copy-and-paste pack. Follow-up edits reuse an already-prepared, uncommitted build rather than consuming another build number.

## Uploading a checkpoint

Git Commit App uses selected source files plus required version metadata. It commits the build and creates its annotated version tag when you submit the checkpoint. A prepared build is reused rather than incremented twice. The commit message becomes the final build name.

Preparing metadata does not upload it. A source tag also does not automatically produce a compiled game or publish a GitHub Release. GitHub wiki pages are maintained separately from the main project's files.
