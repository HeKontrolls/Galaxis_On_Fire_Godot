# Galaxis on Fire — Godot rebuild

This is a **separate Godot 4.7.2 project** beside the original Pygame game. The original code and saves are not used or modified by this project. Open `project.godot` in the Godot Project Manager, run `open_editor.ps1` to edit, or run `run_godot.ps1` to play.

Swords now perform combat swings only: they cannot mine terrain, harvest scenery/crops or recover placed torches. Grass, dirt, sand, snow and tilled soil have visible mining progress. Hands need eight hits, pickaxes six and shovels three. Pickaxes keep a slow 0.38-second soil recovery regardless of material. Shovels use 0.56 seconds divided by their material's speed multiplier, making even Wood faster on soil than every pickaxe. All seven shovel materials have native Aseprite sprites, recipes, durability, inventory/hotbar support and saved wear. They cannot harvest stone or ores. Grass regrowth and soil watering preserve partial digging progress.

Press F at a Workbench for its own nine-slot ingredient grid, recipe picker, result slot and four stored-output slots. Put ingredients in the grid, choose a recipe and click the Make result slot (or Enter); crafting consumes only that workbench's ingredients. Click an output to take the crafted item. All tool assembly now belongs at the workbench. C opens basic hand crafting, and M opens a nearby workbench. Station and backpack windows can be dragged and closed independently; F or Esc closes the station interaction. Drag stacks between the backpack, station slots and hotbar; right-click moves one item. Stored tools retain their remaining durability, and mining a workbench returns its inputs and outputs. Each placed station has separate contents, including stations on different planets. See [the workbench window](tests/workbench_slots_preview.png).

The backpack now has a **Sort** button beside its page controls. It groups tools, weapons, ores/metals, building materials, plants and food by type and name, resets to the first page and preserves the resulting order in saves. Quantities, tool wear, hotbar shortcuts and station storage are preserved. See [the sorted backpack](tests/inventory_sort_preview.png). `tests/station_storage_sort_check.gd` covers stored crafting, automatic smelting, exact-slot dragging and swaps, independent windows/instances, legacy queues, sorting, saved contents and mining recovery.

Press F at a furnace to open its own draggable slot window beside the backpack. Store ore and alloy materials in its six material slots, Coal or Wood in its fuel slot, and take finished items from its six output slots. Smelting starts automatically from stored materials, with five seconds of initial heating: Copper takes 8 seconds, Iron 11, Gold 9, Netherite Scrap 16 and Netherite Ingot 18 once hot. Wood cooks Corn in 6 seconds. Heat, inputs, fuel, active processing and finished outputs persist per placed furnace and across saves. Furnaces continue while windows are open and while exploring other visited planets; Pause and developer mode stop their timers. Older reserved jobs finish without losing their progress. Mining a furnace drops stored inputs, remaining fuel, finished output and the ingredients of unfinished jobs. See [the dedicated furnace window](tests/furnace_slots_preview.png).

Weather effects blend over twelve seconds, including interruptions and saved transitions. Cloud coverage and tint, sky/ground light, rain, snow, ash, fog, wind strength and rain ambience build or fade together. Rain streaks drift and tilt with the current wind, becoming more slanted during strong gusts and vertical in calm air; roof clipping follows the diagonal streak. Puddle input and crop watering follow actual rainfall intensity. Rocky inner/lava planets map rain to Ashfall, storms to Ember Storms, and fog to Volcanic Haze, with no liquid rainfall or snowfall. Natural mineral seams replace element boxes underground, and fresh planets use angled cave mouths. See [rain blowing left](tests/wind_rain_left_preview.png), [rain blowing right](tests/wind_rain_right_preview.png), and [a transition halfway through](tests/weather_transition_06_preview.png). `tests/mining_furnace_weather_check.gd` covers the combined behavior, including held digging at 30/60/144 FPS and saved furnace/weather progress.

Pickaxes now include all seven tiers from John's table. The main crafting path is Wood -> Stone -> Copper -> Iron -> Diamond -> Netherite; Gold is a fast, fragile sidegrade with Wood's harvest level. Swords and axes retain their Wood/Copper/Iron progression. Base tools cost 6 Planks + 3 Sticks at a workbench. Stone uses the wood pickaxe, 6 Cobblestone and 2 Sticks; Copper uses the stone pickaxe, 6 Copper Ingots and 3 Sticks at a workbench; Iron uses the copper pickaxe, 8 Iron Ingots and 4 Sticks at a workbench. Gold uses a wood pickaxe, 3 Gold Ingots and 3 Sticks at a workbench. Diamond uses an iron pickaxe, 3 Diamonds and 3 Sticks at a workbench. Netherite uses a diamond pickaxe and 1 Netherite Ingot at a workbench. Upgrades consume their predecessor; new tools start at full durability.

Wood and Gold harvest stone, cobblestone and coal. Copper and iron ores require at least Stone/Copper; gold, crystal and diamond require Iron; obsidian and Ancient Debris require Diamond. Failed harvests give a requirement message without drops, progress or wear. Mining recovery is 0.76 seconds divided by the listed multiplier; stone and ore blocks break on the first successful strike, while soil requires repeated strikes; recovery applies to held mining, repeated clicks and tool switching. Pickaxes remain slow at chopping wood. Pickaxe damage is the existing base 7 plus the listed bonus. Each completed block/rock harvest costs one durability; a successful creature hit costs two. Broken tools disappear, and a spare stacked pickaxe has its own durability. Hotbar bars, selected-item captions and backpack tooltips show remaining durability. Chest transfers, trash returns, pickups and saves preserve wear. Older saves initialize existing tools at full durability. Q prioritizes harvest level, then mining speed.

Mining terrain creates visible item pickups that enter the inventory when the player approaches. Pickups persist in Godot saves. Cutting a natural tree detaches every trunk and leaf tile as its own block at its original position; the blocks fall slowly, settle on terrain, and can then be collected as Wood or Leaves. Holding left mouse repeats mining, attacks, harvesting, hoeing, and watering as the cursor moves. Holding right mouse repeats building and planting. Releasing the button, opening a modal window, leaving the planet, or losing window focus stops the held action. Hovering the HUD blocks world actions. Digging still obeys the four block limit.

Water has finite cell volume: it falls through openings, spreads sideways, drains into newly dug space, and settles into pools with partial tile surfaces and ripples. Gravity takes priority over sideways spreading, and fluid moves at most one row per simulation tick. Water and lava render as filled, connected streams with shared pool edges and interpolation between ticks. Small falling amounts become droplets, rather than full-width bright bars. Stable areas sleep, and unchanged visible meshes are reused while the camera scrolls. Placing a solid block displaces water into connected free space; a fully sealed cell rejects the placement. Swimming checks the actual water surface. Compressed water volumes persist in saves; older saves initialize their original full water tiles automatically. Moving streams do not require uploading the terrain texture every tick.

Mixed ocean/continent and island worlds now prepare all sea-connected caves before gameplay starts. The loading screen checks floodable cells in bounded batches and shows progress. Preparation uses the same terrain passage rules as the fluid solver, including chests, plants and crafting stations that can share a water cell. Both tile data and saved water volumes are initialized together, so these objects cannot hide a dry cavern that drains the ocean after landing. Sealed caves and spaces above sea level stay dry; objects, storage, lava and rain-water tags are preserved.

Older ocean saves receive one guarded upgrade using the original seeded terrain as a mask. It repairs original natural connections without filling player-dug terrain or crossing constructed walls into sealed rooms. The preparation version and water amounts are saved; later visits preserve subsequent water changes instead of refilling the sea repeatedly. Water remains finite, and opening a new outlet still allows normal flow. `tests/ocean_loading_check.gd` checks eight passable scenery types, edge/bottom cells, guarded save upgrades, real loading-screen progress and ten generated worlds across both ocean climates. The chest-mouth regression now fills its 1,015 empty cells before play and retains its full surface through 200 solver steps.

Lava uses finite-volume flow with slower updates, a limited downward transfer, and slower lateral spreading than water. It drains through openings, forms slow streams, and levels in pools. Placing terrain displaces lava rather than deleting it. Lava renders with a warm surface gradient and local orange cave light. Contact with even a shallow surface or falling stream deals 20 HP immediately, then every half-second; damage is consistent at low and high FPS. Lethal contact respawns the player, and God Mode prevents damage. Partial lava volumes persist in saves; older lava tile saves initialize full cells automatically.

Water touching lava forms solid obsidian and releases steam, consuming a limited amount of the touching water. Rain landing directly on exposed lava gradually cools and darkens it; sustained rain eventually hardens the exposed surface into obsidian. Storms cool it faster. Roofs block rain cooling, and partially cooled lava slowly reheats after rain stops. Cooling travels with flowing lava and persists in saves. Hover over lava to see its cooling percentage. Obsidian has its own dark glass texture, requires a Diamond or Netherite Pickaxe to mine, drops reusable blocks, and can be assigned to any hotbar slot. Hardening lava moves an intersecting player to nearby free space, or waits if no safe space is available.

Digging is limited to a four block (64 pixel) radius from the player's center to the target block's center. Natural trunks, foliage, and crop stages are passable scenery, including on existing saves. Placed wood, planks, and terrain still block movement. Future plants can use the same non-solid scenery rules.

Farming now follows the Python system. Craft a Hoe and Watering Can at a workbench, or obtain the Farming Kit from F3. Equip the hoe and click exposed grass or dirt to till it. Water the soil with the watering can, assign Crop Seeds to a hotbar slot and select them, and right-click the soil or the air directly above it to plant. Wet soil grows crops faster. Corn has sprout, growing, and mature stages; harvest mature plants with left or right click for 2-4 Corn and 1-3 Crop Seeds. Holding the button repeats farming actions. Rain waters outdoor tilled rows, including planted rows, while roofs protect indoor plots. Growth runs near the player at the original Python attempt rate and probabilities. Soil, crop stages, and growth randomness persist in saves. Older Corn Seed inventory stacks migrate to Crop Seeds, and old saved crop stages remain intact.

Corn is now edible: raw Corn restores 15 HP and Roasted Corn restores 30 HP. Select food from the backpack or an assigned hotbar slot and right-click anywhere in the world to eat; right-clicking its backpack slot also eats one serving. R uses selected food when available, otherwise prioritizes carried Roasted Corn then Corn. Eating at full health keeps the food. Eating shows the food moving toward the player's mouth, green healing text, a brief green health bar, and the actual HP restored. Food tooltips list healing values, and selected food shows use instructions or cooldown status above the hotbar.

To roast corn, stand near a Furnace, open crafting with C, choose the Furnace tab, and cook Roasted Corn using one Corn and one Wood as fuel. Cooking checks the station and all ingredients before spending anything. Harvest notices show exact corn and seed yields. Watering with V now requires an owned Watering Can.

Rain and storms deposit finite water above exposed terrain and constructed roofs. Rain input is about 90% lower than the initial puddle implementation. Collection points hold only a shallow film, and drainage also limits the rain-derived volume across each entire column so continuous runoff cannot keep filling a basin. Settled rain puddles target about 3 pixels of depth in rain and 4 pixels in storms; brief flowing runoff can be deeper. Excess rain drains during wet weather, including flooding already stored in saves, and rain trapped underground seeps away. After rain stops, exposed puddles evaporate gradually, faster by day. Drainage and evaporation remove only tagged rainfall, preserving natural water exactly even when the two mix. Rainwater remains identifiable across save/load. Puddles can hydrate exposed tilled soil.

Snowfall builds a shallow white layer on exposed terrain and constructed roofs, keeping covered ground clear. Accumulation stays while snow is falling; when snowfall ends it gradually melts into finite puddle water, which then evaporates. Lava heat also melts nearby snow. Meltwater uses tagged-water drainage and evaporation, preserving natural lakes. Additional runoff limits apply after fluid flow so an entire melting hillside cannot refill a narrow pit; the protection also persists through save/load. Snow re-settles when its supporting floor is dug or a roof is built. Temporary snow layers persist in saves and remain separate from permanent generated snow/ice blocks.

Underground ores use natural Aseprite mineral seams in stone. Existing ore pickups and backpack icons retain their colored element badges: carbon C 6, copper Cu 29, iron Fe 26, and gold Au 79. Alien crystal remains purple with unknown symbol and atomic number markers. Inventory tooltips spell out the labels. Element identifiers follow [IUPAC](https://iupac.org/what-we-do/periodic-table-of-elements/); colors follow the game's materials.
